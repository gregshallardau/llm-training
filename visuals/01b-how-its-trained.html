Stage 1: Pretraining

Produces → Base Model
The analogy: Lock a brilliant reader in the world's biggest library. Give them one game played a trillion times: cover the next word and guess it. Do that across essentially the whole internet and something remarkable happens — to get good at guessing, the reader is forced to learn grammar, facts, reasoning, and patterns. Nobody teaches it insurance; it reads millions of pages where insurance comes up, and the patterns stick.
Training objective
Predict the next token — self-supervised, no human labels needed
Data scale
Trillions of tokens — much of the crawlable web, books, and code
Example guess
"The client's insurance policy expires on June ___" → 30th
Cost
Thousands of GPUs for weeks–months — the expensive part
Why the knowledge cutoff exists: The reading stops at some date. Ask about anything after that and it's genuinely never seen it — like a colleague who's been on a desert island since January. The base model is fluent and knowledgeable, but not yet helpful. Ask it a question and it might respond with more questions, because that's what internet text does.
🎓

Stage 2: Fine-tuning (SFT)

Produces → Instruct Model
The analogy: The base model is a brilliant graduate who's read everything but never had a job. Fine-tuning is the apprenticeship: show them thousands of worked examples — "here's a request, here's a good response" — until they learn the shape of being useful. Same knowledge as before; brand-new behaviour.
Training data
Curated (instruction, ideal response) pairs — written or vetted by humans
Data scale
Thousands to millions of examples — tiny vs pretraining
What changes
Behaviour: follow instructions, helpful register, respect format — not new knowledge
Cost
Hours to days — cheap relative to pretraining
Example exchange it's trained on:
Request: "Summarise this policy in three bullet points."
Good response: "• Covers… • Excludes… • Expires on 30 June."

Show enough of these and the model stops completing your text and starts helping you. This is also where domain fine-tuning lives — specialise the model on insurance-broking text so it speaks the dialect.
👍

Stage 3: RLHF

Produces → Aligned Model
The analogy: The apprentice is now useful — but useful isn't the same as good. Two answers can both follow the instruction while one is clear and honest and the other is waffly or reckless. Teaching that difference is Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback. Exactly how you teach a person manners: feedback. This is where the model's "personality" comes from.
Step 1
Humans compare pairs of model outputs and pick the better one — thousands of times
Step 2
A reward model learns to predict which outputs humans prefer — turning judgement into a score
Step 3
The LLM is fine-tuned with reinforcement learning (PPO) to score highly on the reward model
Why models feel different
Same base knowledge, different RLHF feedback — different "manners"
The personality insight: Helpfulness, caution, refusal to do harmful things — none of this is baked into the knowledge. It was taught by feedback afterwards. The same underlying knowledge can feel very different across models because they were given different manners during this stage. That's half of what you're choosing between when you pick a model.
